Quick answer: Local SEO helps a small business appear when nearby customers search on Google and Google Maps. Start by completing and verifying your Google Business Profile, make your name, address and phone number identical everywhere, build a service page for each main service, earn steady customer reviews, and track calls and direction requests every month.

How Google Decides Local Rankings

Google explains that local results are based mainly on three things. Understanding them tells you where your effort will pay off.

RelevanceHow well your profile and site match the search
DistanceHow far you are from the searcher or the place searched
ProminenceHow well known and reviewed your business is

You can't change distance. Everything in this guide improves relevance and prominence.

Two Ways to Show Up Locally

A local search usually shows a map with three businesses, then the regular list of websites below it. They are ranked separately, and a good local SEO plan works on both.

The Five Foundations

  1. 01

    Claim, Verify and Complete Your Business Profile

    Choose the most accurate primary category, add every service, real hours, photos and a clear description. Most local wins start here.

  2. 02

    Make Your Business Details Consistent

    Your name, address and phone number (NAP) should read exactly the same on your website, your profile and every directory listing.

  3. 03

    Build One Page per Main Service

    A page for each service, with prices or ranges where you can, the areas you cover and real photos, gives Google something specific to rank.

  4. 04

    Earn Reviews Steadily

    Ask every happy customer, make it easy with a direct review link, and reply to every review. A steady flow beats a sudden burst.

  5. 05

    Add LocalBusiness Schema

    Structured data on your site states your name, address, phone, hours and services in a format search engines read without guessing.

A Simple 90-Day Local SEO Plan

  1. DAYS 1 TO 30

    Fix the Foundations

    Complete the Business Profile, correct NAP on your site and main directories, fix technical problems on the website and add LocalBusiness schema.

  2. DAYS 31 TO 60

    Build Relevance

    Create or improve service pages, add service-area pages only where you genuinely serve, and start weekly profile posts and photos.

  3. DAYS 61 TO 90

    Build Prominence

    Run a review request routine, build citations on industry directories and earn local links from partners, suppliers and community groups.

Local SEO Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stuffing keywords into your business name. It breaks Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended. Use your real-world name.
  • Creating pages for every town nearby. Thin, copied location pages rarely rank and can weaken the site. Build pages only for places you truly serve, with unique content.
  • Buying or filtering reviews. Offering incentives or only asking happy customers to review is against Google's policies.
  • Letting the profile go quiet. Outdated hours, unanswered reviews and no new photos signal a business that isn't paying attention.
  • Not tracking anything. Without monthly numbers you can't tell what is working.
Use the Business Profile Data You Already Have

Your profile's Performance section shows calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches people used to find you. It is the fastest way to see whether local SEO is working.

Common Questions

How Much Does Local SEO Cost for a Small Business?

It depends on competition and how much needs fixing. Many foundations, such as completing your profile and fixing NAP, cost nothing but time. Ongoing professional management is usually a monthly fee.

Can I Do Local SEO Without a Physical Storefront?

Yes. Service-area businesses can hide their address and set the areas they serve in their Business Profile, as long as they meet customers at their locations.

How Many Reviews Do I Need?

There is no magic number. Aim to keep a steady flow of recent reviews and compare yourself with the businesses currently ranking in your map pack.

Is Local SEO Worth It for a Small Business?

For businesses that serve a local area, it is often the most cost-effective marketing channel, because it reaches people who are already searching for your service nearby.
