What Is GEO? How to Get Your Business Cited in AI Overviews and ChatGPT

By Salman Yousufzai, Senior SEO Specialist · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: GEO, or generative engine optimization, is the work of making your website the source that AI tools quote when they answer a question. That includes Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. It builds on normal SEO: pages that rank, load fast and answer questions clearly are the ones AI tools tend to cite.

What Is GEO?

When someone asks Google a question, an AI-written summary called an AI Overview often appears above the regular results. When someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity, they get a written answer with a few sources linked underneath. GEO is about being one of those sources.

You'll also hear two related terms. Here's how they fit together:

TermGoalWhere you show up
SEORank your pages in search resultsThe blue links on Google and Bing
AEOGive the direct answer to a questionFeatured snippets, People Also Ask, voice answers
GEOBe quoted inside AI-written answersAI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity

They aren't separate projects. Good SEO is the foundation, and AEO and GEO are about shaping your content so it's easy to quote.

Why GEO Matters for Your Business

AI answers sit at the very top of the page, above every regular result. If your business is cited there, people see your name before they see a single competitor, even if they never scroll.

It's measurable, too. For a home services company in Manhattan, the blog content I planned earned 12.2K impressions inside Google's AI Overviews in three months, tracked in Search Console. A single guide was responsible for 2,714 of them. That visibility came from ordinary blog posts written to answer real customer questions, not from any special trick.

How AI Tools Choose Which Sources to Cite

No AI company publishes its full formula. But from tracking what gets cited, a few patterns are consistent:

  • They pull from pages that are already indexed and trusted. A page Google can't crawl or doesn't rank is rarely cited.
  • They prefer content that answers directly. A clear one or two sentence answer is easy to lift into a summary.
  • They favor structure. Descriptive headings, short sections, lists and tables make facts easy to extract.
  • They look for specifics. Real numbers, locations, prices and steps beat vague claims.
  • They need to know who you are. Consistent business details across your site and profiles help AI tools connect the dots.

7 Steps to Get Your Business Cited in AI Answers

1. Get the SEO Basics Right First

If a page has a noindex tag, a missing title, no H1 or a slow load time, it's unlikely to rank, and unlikely to be cited. Run any page through the free SEO checker to catch the basics in under a minute.

2. Answer the Question in the First Two Sentences

Open each page or section with the answer, then explain. Compare "Our team has years of experience in many areas of home theater..." with "A dedicated home theater typically costs $10,000 to $100,000 or more, depending on the screen, sound and seating." The second one is quotable.

3. Write Headings That Match Real Questions

Use the words people actually search: "How Much Does a Home Theater Cost?" is far more useful to an AI tool than "Our Pricing Philosophy." Keep one H1 per page and use H2 and H3 headings in order.

4. Add FAQs and Structured Data

A short FAQ section answers the follow-up questions people ask. Structured data (schema), such as FAQPage, Service, LocalBusiness and Article, tells search engines exactly what each part of the page is. It doesn't guarantee a citation, but it removes guesswork.

5. Be Specific

Include the details a summary would want: price ranges, timelines, service areas, steps, and real results. "Most installs take one to three weeks" is useful. "We work fast" isn't.

6. Keep Your Business Details Consistent Everywhere

Your business name, address, phone number and services should match across your website, Google Business Profile, social profiles and directories. Mixed signals make it harder for any system to trust that the details belong to you.

7. Track Your AI Visibility

Watch the AI-related performance data in Google Search Console, and once a month, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the questions your customers ask. Note who gets cited. Over time you'll see which pages earn mentions and what to write next.

What About llms.txt?

An llms.txt file is a plain-text summary of your site placed at your domain root, written for AI tools to read. It's an emerging convention, and major AI search products haven't confirmed they rely on it, so treat it as a quick nice-to-have rather than a ranking factor. It takes ten minutes to write. Common GEO Mistakes

  • Skipping SEO and jumping to "AI tricks." There's no shortcut around being indexable, fast and useful.
  • Burying the answer. Long introductions push the useful sentence too far down.
  • Writing for algorithms instead of people. Stuffed keywords read badly to humans and AI alike.
  • Thin pages. A 150-word service page gives an AI tool nothing worth quoting.
  • Not measuring. Without tracking, you can't tell what's working.

Quick GEO Checklist

  • The page is indexable, fast and has one clear H1
  • The first two sentences answer the main question
  • Headings use the words customers search
  • There's a short FAQ section with structured data
  • The page includes specific numbers, steps or locations
  • Business details match across the web
  • AI visibility is checked monthly

GEO Questions Answered

Is GEO Replacing SEO?

No. GEO builds on SEO. AI tools mostly cite pages that are already indexed, trusted and well structured, so strong SEO is still the foundation.

How Long Does GEO Take to Work?

Once a page is indexed and ranking, clearer answers and structure can earn citations within weeks. New sites usually need a few months of SEO first.

Can Small Businesses Do GEO?

Yes. Local businesses often have an advantage, because they can answer specific questions about their services and area better than national sites.

Does Schema Guarantee My Content Will Be Cited?

No. Structured data helps search engines understand your page, but citations depend mostly on the content being clear, specific and trustworthy.
