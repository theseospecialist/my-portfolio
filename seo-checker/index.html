The SEO Terms in Your Report, Explained

Every finding uses the real SEO term, so you can discuss it with any developer or agency. Here is what each one means.

<title>

Meta Title

The clickable headline Google shows for your page, and one of the strongest on-page ranking signals. The checker reports its length and flags titles over 60 or under 30 characters.

<meta name="description">

Meta Description

The short summary under the title in search results. It does not affect rankings directly, but it shapes whether people click. Google shows about 155 to 160 characters.

<link rel="canonical">

Canonical Tag

Tells Google which URL is the main version of a page, so copies with tracking parameters or different paths do not split ranking signals.

<h1>

H1 Tag

The main heading on the page. One clear H1 states the page topic for visitors, Google and AI tools. H2 and H3 tags then structure the sections.

<meta name="robots">

Meta Robots and Noindex

Instructions for search engines. A noindex value keeps a page out of Google entirely, and nosnippet keeps it out of snippets and AI Overviews.

JSON-LD

Schema Markup

Structured data that states facts in a machine-readable way: your business, services, FAQs, articles and reviews. It makes rich results possible.

og:title, og:image

Open Graph Tags

Control the title, description and image shown when a page is shared on LinkedIn, Facebook, WhatsApp and Slack.

<img alt>

Image Alt Text

A text description of each image for Google Images and screen readers. Missing alt text makes images invisible to both.

/robots.txt

robots.txt

A file at the root of your site that tells crawlers which paths they may visit. One wrong line can block Google from an entire site.

/sitemap.xml

XML Sitemap

A list of the URLs you want search engines to find. The checker reads it and samples up to 10 pages for site-wide problems.

LCP, INP, CLS

Core Web Vitals

Google's page experience metrics. Good scores are LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds and CLS under 0.1.

200, 301, 404

HTTP Status Code

The server's answer when a page is requested. 200 means OK, 301 means moved, and 4xx or 5xx errors stop a page from being indexed.

How the SEO Score Works

The SEO score starts at 100 and drops for every issue found, weighted by severity. A single critical issue, such as a noindex tag on an important page, costs far more than several low-priority ones.

  • CriticalStops the page from ranking at all, such as a noindex tag or an HTTP error.
  • HighSeriously limits rankings or clicks, such as a missing H1 tag or missing canonical tag.
  • MediumHolds the page back, such as a meta title over 60 characters.
  • LowWorth tidying, such as missing Open Graph tags.

Questions About the SEO Checker

Is This SEO Checker Free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no sign-up and no limit on how many pages you check.

What Does the SEO Checker Test?

It reads the live page and reports the meta title, meta description, canonical tag, H1 and H2 tags, meta robots, viewport, HTML lang, Open Graph tags, schema markup, image alt text, word count, internal links, HTTP status, HTTPS, robots.txt and XML sitemap. It also runs Google PageSpeed Insights for Core Web Vitals.

How Is the SEO Score Calculated?

Every issue has a severity: Critical, High, Medium or Low. Serious issues lower the score much more than minor ones, so a page with a few critical problems scores poorly even if many smaller checks pass. 85 and above is good, 60 to 84 needs work, and below 60 means problems are likely holding the site back.

Does It Check My Whole Website?

It checks the page you enter in depth, then samples up to 10 more pages from your XML sitemap to spot site-wide problems such as duplicate meta titles, noindex pages and broken URLs. A full SEO audit crawls every page and also covers backlinks, rankings and competitors.

Why Does It Say a Site Blocked the Reader?

Some websites block automated tools. When that happens, the checker uses Google's Lighthouse audits instead, so you still get a report, but the tag table, word count and heading outline are not available.

Does It Check Whether AI Tools Can Find My Site?

This tool focuses on Google SEO. The separate free AI Visibility Checker tests whether ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews can reach, read and quote your pages.

Do You Store the URLs I Check?

No. This site does not save the addresses you check or the results.
